Fourteen people, ten projects

Northgate's data office is 14 FTE - and the portfolio asks for more than 14 FTE can give. Tonight is the leader's people session: the shape of a working data office role by role, when to hire vs contract vs upskill (including literacy training as a genuine capacity lever), who owns pipelines vs models vs dashboards vs governance so the one-A rule survives at org scale, and the capacity math that separates plans from wishes. The number that matters: 14 people at roughly 70% focus is about 10 real FTE. Plan for 14 and you have already failed - you just do not know it yet.

Part 1 · covers the 14-FTE org + hire vs contract vs upskill

The shape of a data office 8 min live

Northgate's year-one data office is 14 FTE: a Head of Data, a PMO lead (you, if you are taking the practitioner track), one platform engineer, four data engineers, three analytics engineers on BI, two data scientists, one ML engineer, and a governance lead. Notice the ratios: engineering-heavy, one lonely ML engineer, and governance funded from day one rather than bolted on after the first incident. That shape is deliberate - it mirrors the dependency ladder from a2: you cannot analyze what nobody piped, and you cannot deploy models on data nobody governs.

LiveHire, contract, or upskill - the three-way call5 min▶

Every capacity gap gets one of three answers, and the signal is the shape of the need, not the loudness of the ask:

  • Hire when the need is permanent and recurring - a capability the office will exercise every quarter for years. Slow (months to productive) but compounding. Northgate's four data engineers are hires: pipelines never stop needing owners.
  • Contract when the need is a spike or a one-off - the cloud landing zone build, a security review, a migration. Fast and expensive, and the knowledge walks out the door, so pair every contractor with a named internal shadow.
  • Upskill when the skill is adjacent to what the team already does - and when the real bottleneck is the BUSINESS side. Data literacy training for store managers and AI literacy training for head office are catalog services precisely because every self-serve user is an ad-hoc request that never reaches the team. Training is capacity building, not a perk.
Real world

The cheapest FTE Northgate never hired. Month 3: regional managers file dashboard requests faster than three BI engineers can clear them. The instinctive fix is "hire a fourth." Instead, the data literacy course teaches forty store managers to answer their own top ten questions in self-serve BI - and the request queue drops by roughly a third. The BI team gets its month back without a single requisition. When training is on the roadmap as a capacity lever, headcount conversations change shape.

Self-studyThe two roles execs mix up3 min read▶

Two pairs of roles get conflated in steering meetings, and each mix-up costs real money:

  • Head of Data vs PMO lead. The Head of Data decides WHAT the office does - owns the cut line, kills projects, faces the board. The PMO lead runs HOW it flows - cadence, visibility, escalation, money hygiene. Give the PMO the cut line and you get a bureaucrat with enemies; give the Head of Data the status mechanics and strategy time disappears into spreadsheets.
  • Governance lead vs data stewards. The governance lead (1 FTE, central) writes policy and runs the quality scorecard. Stewards sit IN the business domains, part-time, applying policy to their own data. Central governance without domain stewards is a documentation project; b4 wires this into a proper RACI.
Self-studyReading the ratios - why 4 : 3 : 2 : 13 min read▶

The delivery ratios (4 data engineers : 3 analytics engineers : 2 data scientists : 1 ML engineer) encode a year-one truth worth saying out loud in steering:

  • Engineering outnumbers science on purpose. Year one is mostly plumbing: landing zone, lakehouse, validated pipelines. Data scientists without piped, governed data are expensive people waiting politely.
  • The BI three are the visible layer. Dashboards and the exec scorecard are what the business SEES - understaff this and the whole office looks slow, whatever engineering ships.
  • One ML engineer is a year-one bet, not an oversight. Two model-bearing projects plus a pilot is the most one person can shepherd to production - Part 3 shows it is already too much if all three run at once.
  • The ratio shifts in year two. As the platform stabilizes, run work grows and the science share can grow with it - which is why headcount plans are re-cut annually, not carved in stone.
Part 2 · covers asset ownership + the one-A rule at org scale

Who owns what - one A per asset 7 min live

RACI's iron rule - exactly one Accountable per deliverable - scales up from documents to whole asset classes. Pipelines, models, dashboards, governance: each needs ONE named accountable role, or every incident becomes a negotiation. The failure mode at org scale is not "nobody owns it" - it is two teams who both sort-of own it, each assuming the other has the pager.

Read the grid twice: once down the A column (every asset has exactly one), once across the ML row - the scientists BUILD models, but the ML engineer is accountable for them IN PRODUCTION. That handoff line, written down, is what stops "the model broke" from becoming a two-team blame loop.

The one-A test for leaders. Point at any asset in the portfolio and ask "who is accountable?" One name, instantly, from anyone you ask = healthy. A pause, a committee, or two names = this session's homework. Full RACI mechanics live in b4 on the practitioner track.
Self-studyWhen two teams both own the dashboard3 min read▶

The classic org-scale RACI smell: the BI team "owns dashboards" and the data science team "owns their model's dashboard." Both true-ish, so the forecasting dashboard has two As - which means zero. It breaks on a Saturday; each team assumes the other saw the alert; the CFO sees stale numbers Monday.

  • Fix by asset, not by tool. The rule "Analytics/BI is accountable for every dashboard executives see, whatever produced the numbers" gives one A. The DS team becomes Responsible for the model behind it, Consulted on the display.
  • Publish the grid. Ownership that lives in people's heads reorganizes itself every time someone is on leave. One page, on the wall next to the portfolio kanban.
  • Escalation is part of R&R. Each A gets a named escalation path - the a5 steering session builds the ladder above them.
Part 3 · covers the focus factor + Brooks's law

Capacity math - the honest kind 7 min live

A person is not 1.0 FTE of project capacity. Meetings, support, interviews, leave, the run work that keeps yesterday's deliveries alive - a realistic focus factor is around 70%. So Northgate's 14 people are about 10 real FTE against 10 projects, which is why the in-flight limit exists. And when a project runs late anyway, the tempting fix - add people - has a name and a warning label: Brooks's law (The Mythical Man-Month, 1975): adding manpower to a late software project makes it later.

LiveWhy 3 projects per person is a plan to fail4 min▶

The allocation matrix says the ML engineer gives 0.4 + 0.6 + 0.5 = 1.5 FTE. The engineer has about 0.7. Three things happen, all bad:

  • Context switching taxes what little there is. Every project swap costs re-immersion time, so the real output is even less than 0.7 - split three ways, each project gets a distracted fraction.
  • Every project inherits the same single point of failure. One resignation, one illness, and three charters slip at once. The org chart flagged this box for a reason.
  • The slip is invisible until it is triple. Each PM sees "my ML task is a bit late" - only the portfolio view (the kanban, the allocation matrix) reveals the shared cause.
Real world

How the overallocation actually surfaces. Northgate's steering meeting, month 4: forecasting is amber, the agentic pilot is amber, segmentation's model task just slipped. Three separate excuses on three separate slides - until the PMO lead overlays the allocation matrix and one column glows. The fix was never "push the teams harder"; it was resequencing the agentic pilot to Q3 (which the a2 roadmap wanted anyway) and contracting model-deployment help for one quarter, with the ML engineer as the named shadow.

Self-studyBrooks's law - the hiring caution, quantified3 min read▶

"Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later" - Fred Brooks, 1975, still undefeated. Two mechanisms do the damage:

  • Ramp-up drains the veterans. New joiners produce little for weeks while consuming the time of exactly the people who were already behind - the project slows down before it speeds up, and "before" is the part you cannot afford.
  • Communication paths explode. Paths grow as n(n-1)/2: a team of 4 has 6, a team of 6 has 15. More of everyone's focus factor goes to coordination - the 0.7 quietly becomes 0.6.

The leader's translation: headcount is a fix for NEXT year's capacity, never for THIS quarter's slip. The simulator's crash button (a6/b5) plays this out: adding people to late projects adds about a month and 15% cost to each one. Mid-year rescue options are the demo's trio - resequence, contract a bounded gap, or descope - not a hiring spree.

Self-studyThe allocation matrix as a standing artifact3 min read▶

Tonight's demo builds the matrix once - the win is keeping it alive. Three habits turn a spreadsheet into an instrument:

  • Re-cut it quarterly, with the roadmap. Allocations follow sequencing: when the agentic pilot moves to Q3, the ML engineer's row changes on the same day, in the same meeting.
  • New project? Show the row it eats. Every mid-year addition must name whose 0.7 it comes from. "We'll absorb it" is the sentence that turns 0.7 into 0.9 into burnout - the matrix makes absorption visible and therefore refusable.
  • Read it next to the kanban. The kanban says WHAT is in flight, the matrix says WHO is carrying it. Amber projects with an overallocated shared person are one problem, not three - the b10 capstone binds both into the year-one PMO pack.
Build-along

Allocate 14 people, find the break ★ 18 min · everyone allocates

The allocation matrix: 14 FTE down the side, 10 projects across the top, fractions in the cells. Fill it for Northgate's Q1-Q2 and find where the plan quietly asks for people who do not exist.

Set each row's budget. Every person's cells may sum to at most 0.7 - not 1.0. Write the 0.7 ceiling at the end of every row before allocating anything. This one habit kills most fantasy plans at birth.

Allocate the obvious rows. Data engineers to the lakehouse and pipelines, analytics engineers to reporting and the scorecard, platform engineer to the landing zone. Watch how fast 0.7 fills up once run-work reality is respected.

Find the hot column. Sum each PERSON's row. At least one row breaks - the ML engineer carrying segmentation, forecasting, and the agentic pilot lands near 1.5 against 0.7. Circle it.

Fix it without Brooks. Three legal moves: resequence (park the agentic pilot to Q3 - the dependency ladder approves), contract a bounded gap (with a named internal shadow), or descope. "Hire someone by March" is not on the list - say why, citing ramp-up and n(n-1)/2.

Check the upskill lever. Which allocations exist only because the business cannot self-serve? Tag the cells that literacy training (services #20 and #27) would hand back - that is the training budget's business case, in FTE.

★ What you walk out with tonight. A filled allocation matrix with every row capped at 0.7, one circled overallocation, and a fix that survives Brooks's law - the exact artifact the b10 capstone builds into the year-one PMO pack.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why does Northgate plan its 14 people as roughly 10 FTE of project capacity?

The focus factor is about what every person's week actually contains, not about roles or attrition. Rows in the allocation matrix cap at 0.7 - plans built on 1.0 per head are fantasy with a spreadsheet.

2 · The forecasting project is late. Why does Brooks's law warn against adding two engineers now?

"Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later" (The Mythical Man-Month, 1975). Headcount fixes next year's capacity, not this quarter's slip - mid-year the legal moves are resequence, contract a bounded gap, or descope.

3 · Store managers flood the BI team with dashboard requests. What signals upskill rather than hire?

Hire for permanent recurring capability, contract for spikes, upskill when the bottleneck is the business side's ability to self-serve. Data literacy and AI literacy training are catalog services because training IS capacity building.
