Two pots of money, one honest question

Northgate's data office runs on two pots: the $2,700k change budget across the 10 portfolio projects, and the $1,100k run budget for platform, licences, and managed services. Mix them and overruns hide; keep them apart and every dollar has a question attached. Part 1 · covers PV / EV / AC worked by hand on the lakehouse

Three letters that end status theater 9 min live

Earned value asks three questions any sponsor understands. PV (planned value): what work SHOULD be done by now, in dollars? EV (earned value): what work IS done, valued at plan price? AC (actual cost): what did we actually spend? "Percent complete" is an opinion. EV is the same opinion forced to carry a dollar sign - and dollars can be divided, compared, and forecast.

LiveThe month-3 arithmetic, by hand6 min▶

The lakehouse: BAC $480k, 6 months, planned as $80k of value per month. At the end of month 3:

  • PV = $240k - three months of plan, 3 x $80k.
  • EV = $215k - the milestones actually finished, valued at their PLAN price (schema done, ingestion done, half the warehouse models - not a gut-feel percent).
  • AC = $250k - what the ledger says left the building.

Now the whole method is six divisions and subtractions:

  • SV = EV - PV = 215 - 240 = -$25k · behind schedule by $25k of work.
  • CV = EV - AC = 215 - 250 = -$35k · over cost by $35k.
  • SPI = EV / PV = 215 / 240 = 0.90 · delivering 90 cents of planned work per planned dollar of schedule.
  • CPI = EV / AC = 215 / 250 = 0.86 · every real dollar buys 86 cents of work.
  • EAC = BAC / CPI = 480 / 0.86 = $558k · if spending efficiency stays as-is, this is the real price.
  • VAC = BAC - EAC = 480 - 558 = -$78k · the overrun, named, three months early.
Real world

"We're 50% done" met the ledger. Northgate's lakehouse PM genuinely believed month 3 meant halfway - the calendar said so. EVM disagreed: EV said 45% of the value existed, AC said 52% of the money was gone. Nobody was lying; the ingestion rework just never made it into anyone's mental percent. The number did the confronting so the people did not have to - and the fix (descope two nice-to-have marts, protect the critical path) happened in month 3, when it cost a conversation instead of a quarter.

LiveTraffic-light reporting: EVM for people who will not read EVM4 min▶

Execs do not want six formulas - they want lights. The course convention (same one the a3 leader session teaches from the steering chair):

LightSPI / CPI bandWhat the PMO says
🟢 Green0.95 or above"On plan - nothing to discuss."
🟡 Amber0.85 to 0.95"Drifting - here is the cause and the correction underway."
🔴 Redbelow 0.85"Off plan - decision needed: descope, re-baseline, or add money."

The lakehouse at CPI 0.86 is amber, one bad month from red - which is exactly the sentence that goes on the steering slide. Two rules keep the lights honest: the light is COMPUTED from SPI/CPI, never hand-picked; and an amber must always carry a cause and a correction, or it is just a mood.

Watch the trend, not the light. A project that goes 0.98 → 0.93 → 0.88 across three reviews is a red story wearing amber. Report the three-month arrow next to every light - direction is the leading indicator, the band is the lagging one.
Part 2 · covers FinOps levers, token metering, workflow automation ROI

The run-side leaks: cloud and tokens 7 min live

EVM watches the change pot. The $1,100k run pot leaks differently: nobody overspends it in one decision - it dribbles out through idle clusters, oversized warehouses, on-demand pricing for steady loads, and GenAI calls nobody is counting. FinOps in one sentence: tag everything, rightsize, commit to what is proven, and watch unit costs - and GenAI adds a fourth meter, tokens, that most orgs discover on an invoice.

LiveThe token meter: what the agentic pilot taught the hard way4 min▶

In the b5 simulator, the agentic pilot (#30) ran 20% over on unmetered token spend - the only overrun in the portfolio that no EVM report would catch early, because token cost is a RUN-shaped leak inside a CHANGE-shaped project. The controls, all cheap, all pre-launch:

  • A token budget per workflow, per day - agreed at charter time, not discovered at invoice time.
  • Metering wired in from call one - every agent call logs workflow, tokens in/out, and model, to a dashboard someone actually owns.
  • Alerts at 80% of budget - so the conversation happens while there is still budget to converse about.
  • An eval gate before scale-up (#31) - the eval framework decides whether more tokens buy more value, or just more retries on a task the agent cannot do.
Real world

The retry loop nobody billed for. Northgate's pilot agent hit a malformed supplier file, failed to parse it, and - being a diligent agent - retried. And retried. Every retry was a full-context call, and nothing in the architecture said stop. Metering with an alert would have paged someone at 80% of the day's budget; instead the team learned about it the way most teams do, from finance, weeks later. The fix took an afternoon. The lesson is the order: meter FIRST, then launch.

Self-studyWorkflow automation ROI: the honest denominator3 min read▶

Workflow automation (#29) is where cost control goes on offense - but only if the math includes ALL the costs. The frame:

  • Value per year = hours the workflow saves x loaded hourly rate of the people saved (loaded = salary + overhead, not base pay).
  • Cost = build cost (the project) + run cost per year (licences, tokens, hosting, AND the maintenance hours - automations are pets, not statues).
  • Payback = build cost / monthly net saving. Under a year: strong. Over two: ask why this one.

Two honesty rules. First, hours saved only count if they are REDEPLOYED - "capacity freed" that nobody re-tasks is a feel-good zero. Second, token spend goes in the run line at the metered rate from lever 4, not at the optimistic pilot-week number. An automation that only pays back on unmetered assumptions is a cost center with good PR.

Part 3 · covers the variance review + the buffer drawdown log

Forecast at completion, and who may touch the buffer 5 min live

EAC turns EVM from a report card into a forecast: EAC = BAC / CPI says "at today's efficiency, this is the real price." The portfolio answer to a bad EAC is not padding every line item - it is a single visible buffer (a3's rule: 10% contingency plus a monthly variance review beats padded lines) and a drawdown log that records every dollar that leaves it. In the simulator, this pairing is the budget-buffer lever: portfolio variance +12% → +1%.

LiveThe monthly variance review - a 45-minute agenda that pays for itself4 min▶

One meeting a month, all 10 projects, five fixed items:

  • 1 · The lights (5 min) - SPI/CPI traffic lights for all 10, with three-month arrows. Greens get zero airtime.
  • 2 · Exceptions (15 min) - every amber and red: cause, correction, owner, date. No cause-and-correction means the item is not ready for the meeting.
  • 3 · EAC movement (10 min) - which forecasts moved since last month, and why. A quietly creeping EAC is the portfolio's smoke detector.
  • 4 · Buffer requests (10 min) - formal asks against the drawdown log, decided in the room.
  • 5 · Decisions logged (5 min) - who decided what, written before anyone leaves.

The buffer drawdown log itself is six columns: date · project · amount · reason · approved by · buffer remaining. Three release rules keep it honest: buffer pays for materialized risks, never scope additions (new scope = new charter, the b1 move); every release is visible at steering; and a second release to the same project triggers a re-baseline conversation, not a third release.

When to release, in one test: "Did something we listed as a risk actually happen?" Yes → buffer exists for exactly this, release it without drama. No, someone just wants more scope → charter it as new work with new money. The log turns that test from a negotiation into a lookup.
Build-along

Run the month-6 variance review ★ 22 min · everyone builds

You are the PMO lead. It is the end of month 6 on the live portfolio, and the variance review starts now - the same 10 projects the b5 simulator scores, worked with tonight's tools.

Light the board. Compute SPI and CPI for the lakehouse from the month-6 figures handed out (same method as the month-3 worked example) and assign lights to all 10 projects from their SPI/CPI pairs. Greens go to the parking lot immediately.

Work the exceptions. For each amber and red, write the one-line cause and the one-line correction. The forecasting project stays amber by design - it carries model risk no budget move fixes (b5's honesty rail); practice saying "re-baselined, monitored" instead of pretending green.

Move the forecasts. Recompute EAC = BAC / CPI for the two worst CPI projects and VAC = BAC - EAC for each. Say the VAC sentence out loud, with the month attached: "at current efficiency, this lands $X over, in month N."

Judge two buffer requests. One is a materialized risk (the data dump that turned out unusable in b2 - discovery risk, listed at charter). One is scope creep wearing a risk costume ("execs now also want mobile dashboards"). Release one, charter the other. Log both in the drawdown log.

Check the token meter. Pull the agentic pilot's per-workflow token report against its daily budget. Set the 80% alert if it is missing. This is the once-a-month glance that replaces the invoice surprise.

Timebox the greens to zero. The whole review earns its 45 minutes by spending them ONLY where the lights are not green. If your review discusses healthy projects, it is a status meeting in a variance costume.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Month 3 on the lakehouse: EV $215k, AC $250k. What is CPI, and what does it mean?

CPI = EV / AC = 215 / 250 = 0.86. Option B computes SPI (EV / PV, the schedule index) - the classic mix-up. Below 1.0 means over cost, and 0.86 is amber trending red.

2 · Same project: BAC $480k, CPI 0.86. What does the lakehouse really cost if nothing changes?

EAC = BAC / CPI (the typical case): dividing by an efficiency below 1.0 makes the forecast GROW. VAC = BAC - EAC = -$78k names the overrun three months before the ledger does.

3 · The agentic pilot ran 20% over on token spend in the simulator. What is the control that would have caught it?

Buffer absorbs materialized risks - it does not detect leaks, and unmetered spend just drains it silently. Metering + budget + alert is lever 4: cheap, pre-launch, and it turns the invoice surprise into a Tuesday ping.
